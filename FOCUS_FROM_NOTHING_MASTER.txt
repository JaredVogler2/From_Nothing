>>> S01 | Product mission and all required constraints <<<
# FOCUS rebuild assignment


Build FOCUS from scratch to schedule **all aircraft with work in process and all applicable jobs**, using actual named workforce, qualifications, authoritative per-line calendars, task dependencies, rework, shortages, quality holds, aircraft economics, and stable commitments. Optimize shared capacity across the entire fleet rather than independently optimizing each aircraft. Build both the specified Flask iOS and Classic experiences while replacing inconsistent scheduling, publication, and scoring authorities.

The user's requested features are normative for this rebuild:

- Task precedence; rework predecessor injection; late parts linked to the root SOI, with root work succeeding the part availability milestone.
- Certification eligibility, multi-certification supersets, expiration filtering, team/shift pure pools, no cross-team borrowing, and mechanic team inference stored in SQL Server.
- Flow-budget duration and crew demand from `FOCUS_FLOW_BUDGET`, including `TOTAL_MECHANICS`; P75 rework estimates for NC/PU/CI; SQL descriptive statistics for P55, average, P75, P90, with future policy toggles.
- Forty-five minutes **total** in-process inspection and a separate 45-minute final inspection before successors become workable.
- Separate control-station earliest, deadline, and gate dates; authoritative bounded per-line `FOCUS_CDAY_OP_PLAN` working days.
- Three shifts, regular working time plus at most 60 minutes allowance, shift 3 on a nonworking date only when the next date is working, distinct activation quota `floor(pool_size * 0.85)`, per-person load limits, and no new start after `shift_effective - 30`.
- Actual time and capacity for aircraft changeovers, aircraft affinity, fewer-certification crew preference and rare-certification preservation.
- Committed-first dispatch, incumbent slot and whole-crew defense with two attempts, enabled commitments and a three-calendar-day horizon.
- Parts from `FOCUS_ETRACS_ACTIVE`, ETA floors on all placement scans, and economic dispatch through `EconomicsRegistry.derive_priority_weights`, followed by complete-fleet cost comparison.
- Delivery commitment tracking with two-day hysteresis, immediate seven-day bad news, and sustained persistence.
- Eight disruption causes, five nominally excusable; effort-weighted scoring; a separate once-only OOS penalty; points, badges, streams, experience, and progression.
- Digital twin by team, shift, and aircraft line; behind-station value with weight 35, five-day cap, and two-day grace with explicit date basis.
- Fixed ten-person fictitious MHE/PSC/TST support pools with the union of team-required certifications in an explicitly modeled nonproduction planning mode.
- Optional grounded explanations based on mock, recorded, and BCAI providers with strict pipeline validation.
- The nine named checks: duration, precedence, workday, shift fit, full crew, non-overlap, load cap, activation quota, parts ETA. Keep these IDs stable and extend their coverage.
- An in-progress successor **never voids incoming precedence from incomplete predecessors**. Record actual history and withhold unsafe continuation; a scoring penalty cannot authorize work.


The scope is all aircraft with applicable work in process and all jobs needed
to explain or satisfy their dependencies. Never use a fixed top-50 production
cap. Current source data includes150 distinct lines with non-COMPLETE master
statuses; that broad census is not proof that every one is an active in-flow
aircraft. Preserve and reconcile all observed work; narrower operational scope
requires an explicit source-backed scope receipt, not an economics mapping.

Separate four products: complete inventory, feasible candidate forecast,
accepted immutable plan, and currently executable next action. A conditional
part ETA or virtual support pool can appear in a labeled forecast. Neither
proves a named mechanic is physically available or a part actually received.

Application behaviors:
- Team leads see qualified feasible next jobs with full crews and explanations.
- Mechanics see exact chronological work, transfers, holds and overtime.
- Management sees full fleet completion, costs, risk, bottlenecks and commitments.
- IE reviews standards/crew/data corrections without rewriting issued goals.
- Supply chain sees shortage/root/descendant impact and ETA versus readiness.
- Quality sees sourced inspection holds and qualified inspector competition.
- Performance uses frozen standard effort and reviewed relief, with separate OOS.
- Twin/scenarios/explanations/recognition are shared projections, never a second
  scheduling, completion, qualification or scoring authority.
