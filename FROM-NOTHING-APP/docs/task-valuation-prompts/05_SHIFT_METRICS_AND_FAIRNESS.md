# Prompt 05 — Shift Performance, Score Incentives and Operational Fairness

## Core question
Should the same number that prioritizes task dispatch also be the number a team "earns" and on which its performance grade depends?

Investigate separations among:
- standardized production output;
- schedule execution/attainment versus approved, frozen obligations;
- adherence to planned versus off-plan work;
- weighted priority execution and critical-task completion;
- observed downstream unlocks / forecast changes;
- quality, rework and reversals;
- schedule loading/plan feasibility (planning accountability, not necessarily team accountability);
- predicted fleet economic/throughput benefit.

Audit existing frozen goal B0, amendments B, independently reviewed excuses E, verified planned output Cp, off-plan U, OOS adjustment O, and the score formula. Identify whether current adherence/attainment combination can hide a large execution shortfall. Do not mistakenly include unverified work as earned.

## Adversarial cases
Simulate and compare:
1. Team A earns 100% on a light, easy-to-schedule shift; Team B earns 85% on a bottleneck-laden shift.
2. A small team has a high priority score but few standardized hours; large team completes many essential routine tasks.
3. An operator performs easy low-value work to maximize raw points.
4. An operator delays ordinary tasks until they become urgent, artificially inflating values.
5. The scheduler updates points during the shift: moving denominators and retroactive point changes.
6. A task is blocked by parts or inspection outside the team's control.
7. Work is assigned with insufficient staffing/certifications.
8. Partial milestones, phase crossing, shared crews, duplicate rewards and reopened work.
9. Excess rework or low-quality first-pass execution generates extra rewards.
10. A team hides blockers or manipulates self-excusal decisions.
11. A team follows FOCUS exactly but model assumptions were poor.
12. Schedule amendments for genuine emergency production priorities.
13. Cross-aircraft critical path and unbalanced job assignment.

Specify whether any score is suitable for coaching, dispatch feedback, team comparison, gamification or personnel decision-making. Avoid claiming suitability for employee evaluation without strong validation and appropriate governance.

## Policies to investigate
- Freeze original published shift start commitment, snapshot and task valuation version.
- Allow documented approved additions/relief with separate original-versus-adjusted reporting.
- Preserve verified and reversible earning events; no duplicate credit for a 3-person crew.
- Treat unknown eligibility/observation feeds as unknown, not poor performance or zero.
- Distinguish operational importance and individual worker controllability.
- Ensure not completing high-importance work never silently earns a bonus.
- Check if "critical points completed / critical points scheduled" is less misleading than a weighted unified grade.
- Do not present model-generated predicted dollars as realized operational savings.
